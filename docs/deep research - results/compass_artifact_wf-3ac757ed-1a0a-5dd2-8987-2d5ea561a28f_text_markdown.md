# Can a Trading Firm Let an External AI Quant Researcher Touch Its Data? Security, Procurement, Licensing and Deployment Reality (2026)

**Bottom line:** A professional trading firm can realistically use an external AI quant researcher only if it runs inside the firm's own environment, calls the firm's own LLM endpoint, and never moves licensed market data or strategy IP into the vendor's infrastructure. Multi-tenant SaaS is effectively dead on arrival for the core product, and the binding constraint is less "enterprise security" than exchange and data-vendor licence terms, which in 2025–2026 have started to explicitly restrict AI/ML use and third-party-hosted AI processing.

## TL;DR

- **Deployment verdict:** The only defensible default is "customer-deployed software": the agent plus the research referee/control plane run in the customer's cloud account (VPC/Kubernetes) or on-prem, with LLM calls routed to the customer's own Bedrock/Azure OpenAI/Vertex tenancy or self-hosted open-weights models, and a vendor control plane that sees metadata only. Hosting the customer's data in your SaaS collides with exchange licences (LSE: no data into third-party-hosted AI without approval; CME: derivative works may not go to third parties; EPEX SPOT: no use for training, development, fine-tuning or validation of AI/ML or algorithmic processes without prior written consent) and with DORA/EBA/FCA outsourcing expectations (audit rights, data location, subcontractor chains, exit plans).
- **Biggest threats to the thesis are not security but licensing and in-house competition:** licensed-data terms are tightening explicitly around AI (CME's September 2026 update, EPEX SPOT November 2025 conditions, FISD's March 2026 note that vendors now seek deletion of "model artifacts"), and the most sophisticated buyers are building their own agents: Bloomberg reported in July 2025, citing Man Numeric senior portfolio manager Ziang Fang, that "several dozen signals generated by the AI system have passed Man Group's investment committee and are slated to be deployed in live trading." The realistic wedge is mid-sized systematic funds, energy/commodity desks and smaller quant teams — not the Man/Two Sigma tier.
- **Verdict: MIXED.** The evidence supports a narrow product — deployable, auditable research-control software with an AI proposer — and weakens the idea of a cloud-hosted "AI researcher you hire." For a solo founder it is buildable only if you accept customer-VPC/on-prem deployment from day one, get SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 readiness early, and validate in interviews that customers' own data licences permit an in-house AI agent to process their data.

## 1. Executive conclusion

**What determines whether a firm can use you at all** — ranked by how likely each is to block a deal:

1. **Licensed-data rights (hardest constraint).** Exchange and data-vendor licences govern *where* data may be processed, *by whom*, and increasingly *by what kind of system*. Several major licensors now single out AI. This is a legal constraint that no SOC 2 report fixes. (FACT for the specific clauses below; INFERENCE for the ranking.)
2. **Location of strategy IP.** Alpha models, feature definitions, positions and research notebooks are the firm's crown jewels, and insiders — let alone vendors — are treated as threat vectors: in SEC v. Jian Wu (S.D.N.Y., filed 11 September 2025) the SEC alleges a former Two Sigma researcher "secretly manipulated at least fourteen investment models" between November 2021 and August 2023, causing at least $165m in client harm that Two Sigma repaid, with parallel DOJ fraud charges, and Two Sigma itself paid $90m in SEC penalties over related model-control failures. (FACT for the case; INFERENCE for the attitude.)
3. **Regulatory outsourcing regime of the buyer.** EU banks/investment firms (DORA Article 28–30, EBA outsourcing guidelines), UK FCA-authorised firms (SYSC 8, FG16/5), and US banks (2023 interagency third-party guidance; SR 26-2 model-risk guidance) impose contract clauses, audit/access rights, data-location disclosure and exit plans. Smaller hedge funds and ancillary-exempt energy traders face much lighter formal regimes, but still run vendor DDQs. (FACT.)
4. **LLM data handling.** Solvable today: enterprise endpoints (Bedrock, Azure OpenAI, OpenAI/Anthropic enterprise) offer no-training commitments, private networking, customer-managed keys and (with approval) zero data retention. The problem is not whether it can be done but that the *customer* must own that tenancy. (FACT.)
5. **Vendor viability of a solo founder.** Continuity, key-person risk, insurance and incident response are standard DDQ items; a one-person company will fail some of these outright at banks and large asset managers. (INFERENCE from DDQ content.)

**Architecture consequence:** Design the product so that the vendor never needs custody of customer data, code or model outputs. Everything valuable to the customer stays in the customer's boundary; what you ship is software, methodology, and (optionally) a metadata-only control plane.

## 2. Sensitive asset map

| Asset | Sensitivity | Why | Implication for AI access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha models / signal code | Extreme | Core IP; model integrity is supervised (Two Sigma case: SEC complaint alleges secret changes to "Decorrelation Parameters" across at least 14 models, Nov 2021–Aug 2023, causing at least $165m client harm) — FACT | Agent must never exfiltrate; all writes to model code must be ticketed, reviewed, and attributable. Your referee/audit trail is a *feature* here. |
| Feature definitions & research notebooks | Very high | Encode the research process; notebooks reveal what the firm believes works | Read access needed for usefulness; outputs must stay in customer storage |
| Research memory (tested/failed/survived hypotheses) | Very high (INFERENCE) | This is effectively a map of the firm's edge and dead ends — arguably *more* sensitive than any single model | Your "Research Knowledge System" must be customer-hosted and customer-owned; never pooled across clients |
| Positions, P&L, raw orders/executions | Extreme | Reveal strategy, capacity, crowding; often MNPI-adjacent for counterparties | Default: deny. Grant only aggregated/lagged views for research on transaction costs |
| Internal forecasts (load, weather, fundamentals for energy desks) | High | Proprietary edge for utilities/power traders | Read in scope for energy use cases; outputs stay internal |
| Licensed exchange/vendor data | High legally, variable commercially | Governed by licence; audit risk; fee liability by "unit of count" (see §5) | May only be processed where the licence allows — typically inside customer's controlled environment |
| Alternative data | High legally | Vendor contracts, MNPI and provenance risk (in September 2021 the SEC charged App Annie and its co-founder Bertrand Schmitt in what it called "the SEC's first enforcement action charging an alternative data provider with securities fraud"; App Annie paid a $10m penalty) — FACT | Contract-by-contract permission; AI clauses increasingly present |
| Counterparty information | High | Confidentiality agreements, GDPR where personal data | Out of default scope |
| Market-access infrastructure (OMS/EMS, exchange gateways, credentials) | Extreme | Operational/market-abuse risk | Hard deny; no network path from research sandbox to production trading |

**Implication:** An agent that is "useful" needs read access to rows 2, 3, 5 and 6 at minimum, and write access to research artefacts. That combination is precisely the set of assets firms least want outside their perimeter. (INFERENCE.)

## 3. LLM / cloud policy reality

**FACT — the 2023 baseline was prohibition by default.** In February 2023 Bloomberg reported that Bank of America, Citigroup, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo joined JPMorgan in banning or restricting staff use of ChatGPT; JPMorgan's restriction was described as part of "normal controls around third-party software," Bank of America placed ChatGPT on its list of unauthorised apps, and Deutsche Bank disabled access. The trigger was generic third-party software control, not a specific incident.

**FACT — the 2025–2026 pattern is "enterprise or private deployment, never public."** FISD's Alternative Data Council (March 2026) states that "most investment firms employ enterprise or private AI implementations specifically to address data security and IP protection concerns," distinguishing public tools (higher risk), enterprise versions on private platforms such as OpenAI via Microsoft Azure, and private/proprietary models on firm infrastructure (highest control, highest resource cost).

**FACT — what the major enterprise endpoints actually commit to:**

| Control | Amazon Bedrock | Azure OpenAI | OpenAI API/Enterprise | Anthropic (Claude for Financial Services, July 2025) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Training on prompts | No; not shared with model providers | Not used to train without permission; not shared with OpenAI | No by default for business data | "By default, your data is not used for training our generative models" |
| Retention | Does not store or log prompts/completions | **Default: prompts/completions retained up to 30 days for abuse monitoring**; "Modified Abuse Monitoring" removes storage, via a Limited Access application typically for managed EA/MCA customers | Zero data retention available to "qualifying organizations" | ZDR agreements exist; scope per agreement (third-party reports say feature-limited — UNKNOWN in detail) |
| Private networking | AWS PrivateLink / VPC endpoints | Private endpoints; region-pinned deployments | — | Cloud marketplace procurement (AWS; Google Cloud "coming soon" at launch) |
| Keys | Customer KMS keys | Customer-managed keys | Enterprise Key Management | — |
| Residency | Region-bound unless cross-region inference opted in | Global/DataZone deployment types may process across regions; dedicated regional deployment needed for strict residency | Data residency in multiple regions | — |
| Attestations | SOC 1/2/3, ISO 27001/27017/27018/27701, CSA STAR L2 | Azure compliance scope | SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001/27017/27018/27701 | SOC 2 Type I & II, ISO 27001:2022, ISO 42001:2023 |

**INFERENCE — architecture implications:**
- The right design is **"bring your own model endpoint"**: the customer holds the Bedrock/Azure/Vertex account, the enterprise agreement, the ZDR approval and the keys; your software calls it through a private endpoint. You never become a data processor for prompts.
- Azure's default 30-day abuse-monitoring retention is a common procurement snag for confidential workloads; developers on Microsoft's own Q&A describe it as "incompatible with the confidentiality obligations" of their customers. Plan for customers who need Modified Abuse Monitoring approval or prefer Bedrock.
- Support **self-hosted open-weights models** (vLLM/TGI on customer GPUs) for firms that will not send strategy code to any external model provider, even under ZDR. Evidence that a meaningful share of quant firms require this is thin — UNKNOWN.
- Egress control, secrets management (customer's vault; no vendor-held credentials), per-action audit logs written to customer-controlled storage, and RBAC mapped to the customer's IdP (SSO/SCIM) are table stakes (INFERENCE from BYOC best-practice documentation and DORA/FCA access requirements).

**ANECDOTE — sophisticated firms are building rather than buying.** Man Numeric built AlphaGPT internally; it generates ideas, writes code and backtests, and "several dozen" AI-generated signals have passed Man's investment committee for live trading (Bloomberg, July 2025). The team reported running into "hallucination, lookahead bias, multiple testing" problems (AI Street interview). Man's own paper characterises off-the-shelf LLMs as handling "intern-level work effectively." Bridgewater's AIA Labs appears as a launch customer quote for Claude for Financial Services — i.e., top firms consume raw model access and build the agent layer themselves.

## 4. Procurement / security requirements

### 4.1 Regulatory baseline by jurisdiction

**EU — DORA (applies from 17 January 2025) (FACT):**
- Art. 28(3): in-scope financial entities must keep a **Register of Information** on all ICT third-party contracts and report it to their competent authority — i.e., your customer reports *you* to its regulator.
- Art. 30(2), for every ICT contract: full service description including whether subcontracting is permitted; **locations (regions/countries) where services are provided and data processed/stored, with advance notice of changes**; availability, integrity, confidentiality and personal-data provisions; access, recovery and return of data; service-level descriptions; incident assistance; cooperation with authorities; termination rights; participation in the customer's ICT security awareness training.
- Art. 30(3), for **critical or important functions**: quantitative/qualitative SLAs, notice and reporting obligations, business continuity plans, participation in threat-led penetration testing, unrestricted access/inspection/audit rights, and exit strategies with mandatory transition periods. Microenterprises may delegate audit rights to an independent third party appointed by the provider.
- Art. 29: pre-contract concentration-risk and substitutability assessment.
- **Scope carve-out (FACT, via secondary reproduction of Art. 2(3)(d)):** entities exempted under MiFID II Articles 2 and 3 — including commodity/energy traders using the ancillary-activity exemption — are outside DORA as financial entities. Many utilities' trading arms and commodity houses therefore face lighter formal ICT-outsourcing rules (NIS2 may apply instead — UNKNOWN, not verified).

**EU — EBA Guidelines on outsourcing (EBA/GL/2019/02) (FACT):** apply to credit institutions and CRD investment firms; for critical or important functions require "full"/"unrestricted" audit and access rights for the firm and its competent authority, explicit rules on sub-outsourcing with rights to object, and exit plans. (INFERENCE: the EBA page is now titled "Guidelines on third party risk management," suggesting ongoing revision alongside DORA; check current version before relying on specifics.)

**UK — FCA SYSC 8 and FG16/5 (FACT):** FG16/5 treats cloud and SaaS as outsourcing regardless of IaaS/PaaS/SaaS form; firms must retain "effective access to data" (including system audit trails and logs) and to business premises for the firm, its auditor and the FCA; FG16/5 states encryption keys should be kept secure and accessible to the regulator. SYSC 8.1.9R requires a written agreement allocating rights and obligations.

**US (FACT):**
- **Banks:** the June 2023 Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships lists due-diligence factors including information security, operational resilience, incident reporting, **reliance on subcontractors**, **insurance coverage**, financial condition and key-personnel background; contracts should address audit rights, confidentiality, business continuity, subcontracting and termination.
- **Model risk:** on 17 April 2026 the Fed, OCC and FDIC issued SR 26-2, superseding SR 11-7; it is "most relevant" to banks above $30bn in assets, defines models as quantitative methods applying statistical/economic/financial theory, retains "effective challenge," and states: "Generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving. As such, they are not within the scope of this guidance," while telling banks their governance practices should still determine controls for such tools. An RFI on AI is planned.
- **Investment advisers:** on 12 June 2025 the SEC withdrew its proposed adviser outsourcing rule (along with the predictive-data-analytics/AI conflicts proposal and the adviser cybersecurity proposal). There is currently no SEC rule specifically prescribing adviser vendor due diligence; oversight flows from fiduciary duty, examinations and general compliance-program rules. (INFERENCE: this lowers formal friction for US hedge funds, not practical diligence.)

**INFERENCE — the agentic-AI gap cuts both ways.** SR 26-2 excluding agentic AI means bank-affiliated buyers have no settled supervisory template for your product — procurement committees will default to conservatism. Conversely, a product that makes AI-generated research *auditable* (pre-registration, sealed holdouts, multiple-testing logs, effective-challenge evidence) maps directly onto the model-risk vocabulary the traditional parts of SR 26-2 still use for the *resulting* statistical models.

### 4.2 Practical procurement artefacts

**FACT:** The AITEC-AIMA Illustrative Questionnaire for Due Diligence of Vendor Technology and Cyber Security is the industry template; AITEC reports adoption by over 250 alternative asset managers and 200 vendors.

**Expected artefacts (INFERENCE from the guidance above and vendor trust pages):**
- SOC 2 Type II report (Type I will be accepted only briefly, if at all); ISO 27001 increasingly expected in Europe; ISO 42001 now appearing among AI vendors (Hebbia, Rogo, Anthropic all list it).
- Annual third-party penetration test with executive summary shareable under NDA (Hebbia makes pen-test reports available on request).
- Incident response plan with notification SLAs; business continuity/DR plan; subprocessor list with change notification; DPA (GDPR Art. 28) where personal data is in scope; data-location statement.
- Cyber and professional-indemnity/E&O insurance certificates (named as a due-diligence factor in the US interagency guidance).
- Source-code escrow or deployable-artefact escrow for critical tools (INFERENCE; not directly evidenced in sources gathered).
- Model governance documentation: model cards for any LLM used, evaluation methodology, change-management process, audit-log schema.

### 4.3 Requirements by buyer type (INFERENCE unless stated)

| Buyer | Formal regime | Likely hard requirements | Where a solo founder fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small hedge fund / prop shop (<$1bn, <50 staff) | Light (US: no outsourcing rule; UK/EU: SYSC 8 or AIFMD delegation where relevant) | Data stays in their cloud; no training; references; basic DDQ; often no SOC 2 required for a pilot | Rarely on formal grounds; more on "will you exist in 2 years" |
| Utility / energy trader | Often outside DORA via ancillary exemption; REMIT/market-abuse controls; corporate IT security | On-prem or own-cloud, strict OT/IT segregation, energy-exchange licence compliance (EPEX/EEX) | Licence compliance (see §5) and corporate procurement cycles |
| Bank-affiliated trading desk | DORA/EBA (EU), SYSC 8 (UK), interagency TPRM + SR 26-2 (US) | Register of Information entry, full Art. 30 clauses, audit rights, TLPT participation if critical, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, insurance, financial statements, exit plan | Almost certainly at vendor-viability and insurance/financial-standing checks |
| Large institutional asset manager | UCITS/AIFMD delegation rules, DORA for EU entities, SYSC 8 (UK) | Same as bank tier minus some banking-specific items; heavy AITEC-AIMA-style DDQ | Same as above |

## 5. Licensed-data constraints (critical)

**Headline (INFERENCE):** An external AI vendor generally *cannot* lawfully receive and process a customer's licensed exchange data in the vendor's own infrastructure without the licensor's consent. The customer usually *can* process it with AI tools it controls, subject to non-display licensing and — increasingly — AI-specific clauses. The product must therefore bring the agent to the data, not the data to the agent.

### 5.1 Exchange terms (FACT)

**London Stock Exchange (AI guidance note):** "We license the what, not the how" — existing policies (derived data, non-display, redistribution) apply to AI. Two clauses matter most for architecture:
- "Data should not be consumed into any artificial intelligence solutions which are hosted or operated by a third party unless approved by the London Stock Exchange or Turquoise… where the Customer does not have technical control over the Data in these systems."
- Unit of Count and fees apply "to any Device technically able to access Data, and if the Customer is unable to control Data processed through artificial intelligence solutions, all Devices technically able to access the Data are chargeable." Real-time data must sit behind an entitlement system that permissions, monitors and reports on a user/device basis.

**CME Group:**
- Non-display use — explicitly including "research & analysis" — requires a licence, counted per Application.
- DS-45 notice (agreement updates effective **17 September 2026**; website-display changes 1 January 2027): licensees may "create limited derivative works… solely for its internal business purposes… may only disclose any such derivative works internally and may not distribute any such derivative work to any third party without the prior written consent of CME"; a new prohibition on creating any "Synthetic Feed"; "Licensee undertakes that Licensee Group shall not carry out text and data mining as those terms are defined in EU Directive 2019/790"; delayed and historical data never enter the public domain; a purge obligation on termination with written certification; audits up to 24 months post-termination, and where usage cannot be established, fees on "Presumed Activity" at the highest tier.
- The explicit AI/ML/LLM ban (training, RAG vector databases, synthetic data, "automated generation of trade signals") sits in the *website-display* terms for publicly displayed information, not in the general non-display licence.

**EPEX SPOT (General Conditions, 1 November 2025):** "The Contracting Party shall not use, permit the use of, or make available the Information Products… in whole or in part, for training, development, fine-tuning, validation or enhancement of any artificial intelligence ('AI') system, machine learning model, algorithmic process, whether proprietary or not, unless express prior written consent" is given.

**EEX:** the Internal Usage Licence permits use "exclusively within their company without onward dissemination to external third parties or Affiliated Companies."

**Cboe / FISD derived-data test:** derived data is data that cannot be readily reverse-engineered to recreate the source or used as a reasonable facsimile; derived data containing price-like values for a single instrument is generally fee-liable.

**INFERENCE — what this means:**
- **CME TDM clause is a live ambiguity.** EU Directive 2019/790 defines TDM broadly as automated analysis to generate patterns, trends and correlations — which describes quantitative research itself. It presumably does not override licensed non-display research use, but an AI agent systematically mining CME data is exactly the activity a licensor could contest. UNKNOWN how CME interprets this; ask customers' market-data teams.
- **EPEX SPOT is the sharpest risk for your energy wedge.** Read literally, "validation… of any… machine learning model, algorithmic process, whether proprietary or not" covers ordinary backtesting of a power-trading model on EPEX data, with or without an LLM. Either the clause is not enforced that way, or power desks already hold consents — UNKNOWN. An AI research agent on EPEX data without written consent is plausibly a breach even inside the customer's environment.
- **Vendor-as-processor is the problem, not AI as such.** Even where AI is permitted, putting data in *your* infrastructure makes you a third party receiving redistributed data or a service facilitator, which requires separate licensing (LSE has a Service Facilitator licence category) and may trigger per-device fees on everything that can technically reach the data.
- **Derived outputs must stay internal.** Your research memory, features and signals built from CME data are "derivative works" that may not go to a third party — including you — without consent. Cross-client learning from derived data is off the table.
- **Entitlement integration is mandatory.** The agent must be a named, entitled "user/application" in the customer's market-data inventory so the firm can report it and survive audits.

### 5.2 Alternative data (FACT + INFERENCE)

FISD (March 2026) reports data vendors "expressing heightened concerns" about LLM use: memorisation and redistribution, seat-based licences being bypassed by firm-wide LLMs, fine-tuned models embedding data, substitution risk, and — newly — deletion obligations extending to "model artifacts" such as high-fidelity embeddings for RAG. FISD's buy-side position: firms should certify deletion of raw data and substitute-like artefacts but not be forced to retrain models. FISD also notes contracts "may specify whether the vendor's original data will be confined within the firm's IT environment or transferred to, stored, or processed within the AI provider's systems."

**INFERENCE:** Your research knowledge system and any vector index are exactly the "model artifacts" vendors are targeting. Build per-dataset lineage so the customer can purge everything derived from dataset X on licence termination — this is a sellable feature, not a burden.

### 5.3 Can an external AI vendor legally process a firm's licensed data?

- **In the vendor's SaaS:** generally **no** without licensor approval (LSE explicit; CME derivative-work and redistribution terms; EEX internal-only) — FACT for the clauses, INFERENCE for the conclusion.
- **As software running under the customer's technical control:** generally **yes** for traditional non-display research, subject to non-display licences, unit-of-count reporting and entitlements; **uncertain** where AI-specific clauses exist (EPEX SPOT; CME TDM) — INFERENCE/UNKNOWN.
- **Contractor access by vendor staff** (e.g., remote support viewing data) is itself a potential third-party access issue — design support workflows that never need data access. (INFERENCE.)

Your product is in the second category.

## 7. Recommended default deployment

**Default: Model C (customer-VPC / BYOC) with a "no-data" vendor control plane, plus a Model D/E packaging path for on-prem buyers.** (INFERENCE, based on §3–§6.)

**Reference architecture:**
1. **Data plane (customer AWS/Azure/GCP account, dedicated VPC/namespace):** agent runtime; research referee/control plane (point-in-time data access layer, holdout vault, experiment registry, multiple-testing ledger); research knowledge store; sandboxed compute (ephemeral containers, no internet egress); all logs and artefacts in customer-owned buckets encrypted with customer KMS keys.
2. **LLM access:** customer-owned Bedrock/Azure OpenAI/Vertex deployment via PrivateLink/private endpoint, in the customer's chosen region, under the customer's enterprise agreement and ZDR/modified-abuse-monitoring approvals; pluggable open-weights model option for firms refusing any external provider.
3. **Vendor control plane (your account):** licence keys, release channel, optional *opt-in* health telemetry with metric names only — no prompts, code, data, results or hypothesis text. Pull-based updates (data plane polls outbound) so no inbound ports into the customer's VPC.
4. **Support model:** vendor staff never get data access by default; break-glass access is customer-granted, time-boxed, session-recorded, logged in the customer SIEM.
5. **Identity:** customer IdP (SAML/OIDC + SCIM); agent runs under its own non-human identity registered in the market-data entitlement system.
6. **Exit:** everything the product creates — hypotheses, experiments, evidence, scorecard — is exportable in an open schema; uninstall leaves the customer with a complete research record (supports DORA Art. 30 exit and FISD-style deletion certifications).

**Why this default:** It removes you from most licence redistribution questions (data never leaves the customer's technical control — the LSE test), shrinks outsourcing exposure (you supply software, not a data-processing service — though DORA still treats it as an ICT service contract), and makes the customer bear LLM and compute costs, protecting margins.

**What it costs you:** You must become good at infrastructure-as-code across three clouds, upgrade management across dozens of isolated environments, and debugging without data. For a solo founder, start with **one cloud (AWS + Bedrock)** and one Kubernetes distribution.

## 8. Permission model for the AI researcher

A "mandate" is a signed, versioned policy object approved by a named human (head of research/CRO delegate) and enforced by the control plane, not by the LLM. (INFERENCE — design proposal.)

| Dimension | Default | Escalation path |
|---|---|---|
| **Data scopes** | Allow-list of datasets by ID, each tagged with licence source, permitted uses (research/non-display), AI-permitted flag, retention/purge rule, and PIT availability timestamps. Datasets without an explicit AI-permitted flag are unreadable by the agent. | Market-data/compliance sign-off per dataset; recorded consent reference (e.g., "EPEX consent letter #") |
| **Research universe** | Instrument/asset-class/venue/date-range allow-list; restricted list (MNPI, watch lists) enforced at query level | Compliance approval |
| **Read permissions** | Approved datasets, feature library (read), prior research memory (own mandate only) | Cross-desk memory requires both desk heads' approval (Chinese walls) |
| **Write permissions** | Only to agent workspace: new features, experiment configs, reports. **No writes** to production feature store, model repo main branch, or configs | Pull request into firm's repo reviewed by human; agent cannot approve its own PR |
| **Holdout access** | **None.** Sealed holdouts are evaluated only by the referee on a human-approved pre-registered spec; agent receives pass/fail and pre-agreed statistics, never raw holdout data | Holdout "unsealing" is a logged, one-time human action |
| **Execution sandbox** | Ephemeral containers, CPU/GPU quotas, read-only data mounts, no host access, no persistence outside workspace | — |
| **Network egress** | Deny-all except customer's LLM private endpoint and internal package mirror | No internet by default; web research (if ever) via a separate, data-less agent |
| **Model-provider access** | Only customer-owned endpoints listed in mandate; per-provider data classification (e.g., no raw position data to any external model; code and feature definitions only to ZDR endpoints) | CISO sign-off to add a provider |
| **Approved libraries** | Pinned, scanned package set from internal mirror; no runtime `pip install` | Library request queue |
| **Compute budget** | Hard caps: GPU-hours/day, LLM tokens/day, experiments/week, and **a statistical budget** — maximum number of hypotheses tested against a given dataset/holdout family, feeding the multiple-testing correction | Research head approval |
| **Human approval gates** | (1) mandate approval; (2) hypothesis pre-registration before holdout test; (3) promotion to "candidate signal"; (4) any production deployment — always outside the agent's reach | — |
| **Audit** | Every prompt, tool call, data query, code diff and result hashed and appended to a tamper-evident log in customer storage | — |

**Why this matters beyond security (INFERENCE):** The Two Sigma complaint describes a modeller who used an engineering ticket system to bypass the firm's required review process. A control plane where the agent *cannot* modify parameters outside a reviewed, logged path is the same control that firms now need for humans. That is the strongest "why buy" argument surfaced in this research.

## 9. Likely sales / implementation friction

- **Licence due diligence per customer (INFERENCE):** each deployment requires the customer's market-data team to confirm AI use is permitted for each dataset. For energy desks on EPEX data, that may require obtaining written consent — weeks to months, outside your control.
- **Security review (FACT for content; INFERENCE for effort):** AITEC-AIMA-style DDQ, SOC 2 Type II request, pen-test summary, insurance certificates, subprocessor list. Without SOC 2 Type II you will likely be limited to pilots at small funds.
- **Regulated-buyer contracting (FACT for clause content):** EU banks/investment firms need DORA Art. 30 clauses and a Register of Information entry; if the function is classed "critical or important," add unrestricted audit rights, TLPT participation and exit plans. UK firms add FG16/5 access rights for the FCA.
- **Infrastructure onboarding (INFERENCE):** IAM roles, VPC, GPU quota, LLM endpoint approval (e.g., Azure Modified Abuse Monitoring requires a Limited Access application through a Microsoft account team), data connectors to kdb+/Parquet/Snowflake/Databricks, entitlement registration.
- **Sales-cycle length:** UNKNOWN from desk research. No reliable public figures found for this category; do not plan around a number until interviews give one.
- **Internal politics (ANECDOTE/INFERENCE):** at firms like Man, an internal team already owns "AI research agents"; an external vendor competes with that team's roadmap. At smaller funds, the PM who benefits may not control infrastructure or compliance approval.
- **Proving value without data access (INFERENCE):** you cannot see customer results, so case studies and ROI evidence must come from the customer's own Economic Scorecard — slow to accumulate and possibly not shareable.

## 10. Potential kill conditions

Each is a condition that, if confirmed in interviews, should stop or radically reshape the business. (INFERENCE unless stated.)

1. **Licensed-data kill:** If most target customers' key datasets (exchange data, especially EPEX/CME for energy/futures desks; top alt-data sets) contractually prohibit AI/ML processing or "validation" without consent, and licensors refuse or charge heavily for consent, the agent is useless on the data that matters. *Test:* ask five market-data managers whether their licences allow an in-house LLM agent to read and backtest on exchange data.
2. **In-house build kill:** If the best-resourced buyers (the Man/Bridgewater tier) all build internally and smaller firms lack enough data/research volume to benefit, the addressable market collapses to a thin middle. *Evidence already points to top-tier build (ANECDOTE: besides AlphaGPT, Man built ManGPT, which uses both internal and external LLMs and, per Man's Collier as reported by AI Street, is "used by half the firm on a monthly basis").*
3. **On-prem-only kill:** If most buyers require fully on-prem/air-gapped deployment with self-hosted models, the product becomes enterprise software plus services — low margin, high support — which a solo founder cannot staff.
4. **Vendor-viability kill:** If bank desks and large asset managers (the only buyers with big budgets) require financial standing, insurance limits, 24/7 support and DORA critical-function terms that a startup cannot offer, the realistic market is small funds with small budgets.
5. **Value-without-memory kill:** If customers refuse to let research memory persist (fearing it maps their edge) or demand purge on every licence termination that guts it, the "accumulating junior researcher" value proposition weakens to "a backtest harness with an LLM."
6. **Commoditisation kill:** If Bloomberg (BQuant), LSEG (MCP connectors into Copilot Studio and ChatGPT), Databricks or KX ship "AI research agent inside your data platform" features, the licence-holding incumbents can offer AI on their own data with licensing already solved — a structural advantage you cannot match.
7. **Regulatory-classification kill:** If bank model-risk functions classify the agent itself as a model (or a future agentic-AI guidance does), every deployment triggers independent validation of an LLM system — possibly infeasible for a startup to support.

## 11. Implications for the AI Quant Researcher Thesis

### 1. What the evidence supports
- **Demand for AI-driven quant research is real and already productive at the frontier (FACT/ANECDOTE):** Man Numeric's AlphaGPT has produced signals approved for live trading, and its team explicitly fought lookahead bias and multiple-testing problems — exactly the failure modes your Referee/Control Plane targets.
- **The separation of proposer and referee is aligned with how regulators and firms think about model risk (FACT + INFERENCE):** SR 26-2 retains "effective challenge" and independent validation for statistical models; the Two Sigma case shows firms need tamper-evident, review-enforcing controls on model changes. A deterministic, auditable research control plane is a credible, differentiated product.
- **A technically viable secure deployment pattern exists (FACT):** customer-owned LLM endpoints with no training, private networking, customer keys and ZDR; BYOC/customer-VPC deployment is an established pattern (AlphaSense Private Cloud, Databricks classic, KX, QuantConnect on-prem).
- **Mid-tier and energy/commodity buyers face lighter formal regimes (FACT):** SEC outsourcing rule withdrawn (June 2025); ancillary-exempt energy traders outside DORA.

### 2. What the evidence weakens
- **"External AI researcher you hire" as SaaS:** licence terms (LSE third-party AI hosting clause, CME derivative-work and TDM clauses, EEX internal-only, EPEX SPOT AI prohibition) make vendor-hosted processing of licensed data largely unworkable.
- **Energy/power as an easy wedge:** EPEX SPOT's November 2025 conditions directly restrict AI/ML/algorithmic "development… validation" uses — the heart of the product — unless consent is obtained.
- **Cross-customer learning / network effects:** derived works cannot go to third parties (CME) and research memory is edge-revealing; there is no legitimate path to pooling learning across clients, which removes a classic AI-startup moat.
- **Selling to banks and large asset managers early:** DORA/EBA/FCA/interagency requirements plus vendor-viability diligence make these near-inaccessible to a solo founder.
- **Top-tier firms as customers:** evidence (Man, Bridgewater using raw model access) suggests they build the agent layer themselves.

### 3. What remains unknown and cannot be resolved through desk research
- How licensors *actually interpret and enforce* AI clauses for in-house, customer-controlled agents (CME TDM clause; EPEX "validation of… algorithmic process"); whether consents are routinely granted and at what cost.
- What share of target firms would accept customer-VPC deployment vs demand on-prem/air-gapped with self-hosted models.
- Whether firms will let a vendor's software persist a structured research memory of their edge, and under what ownership/purge terms.
- Real sales-cycle lengths, pilot budgets and willingness to pay at mid-sized systematic funds and energy desks.
- Whether AI-generated research survives prospectively out-of-sample at non-frontier firms (Man's results are self-reported and unaudited).
- Whether internal quant teams see the product as a tool or as a threat to headcount — which determines who champions or blocks it.

### 4. The 5 most important questions to ask practitioners
1. **Market-data/compliance lead:** "If an LLM-driven agent running entirely in your own cloud account read and backtested on your exchange and alt-data feeds, which of your licences would need amending or consent — and have you already asked any vendor (e.g., EPEX, CME) about AI use?"
2. **CTO/CISO:** "What is the minimum deployment you would approve for a vendor tool that reads strategy code and research notebooks — SaaS with ZDR, our VPC with your control plane, our Kubernetes operated by us, or air-gapped? Which LLM endpoints are already approved internally?"
3. **Head of research/CIO:** "Where exactly do research hours go today — idea generation, data wrangling, backtest hygiene, or validation and review? Have you seen false discoveries reach production because of leakage or multiple testing, and what did they cost?"
4. **Head of research/CIO:** "Would you let a tool keep a persistent, structured record of every hypothesis your team tested and why it failed? Who would own it, and what would you require on purge and access?"
5. **COO/procurement:** "What did your last new research-tooling vendor have to provide (SOC 2, ISO 27001, insurance, DDQ, escrow), how long did onboarding take, and what would disqualify a company of one to five people?"

### 5. Verdict: **MIXED**

The evidence does not warrant DO NOT BUILD: the security and LLM-privacy problems are solvable with a customer-deployed architecture, the referee/control-plane concept addresses documented pain (lookahead bias, multiple testing, unreviewed model changes), and there is a reachable buyer segment with lighter regulatory friction. But it clearly **weakens** the framing of an externally hosted "AI junior quant you hire." Build only as **customer-deployed research-integrity software with an AI proposer**, sell first to mid-sized systematic funds and quant teams (and to energy desks only after licence consent is confirmed), and treat kill conditions 1 (licensed-data AI restrictions) and 2 (in-house build) as the first things to falsify in customer interviews — before writing production code.
