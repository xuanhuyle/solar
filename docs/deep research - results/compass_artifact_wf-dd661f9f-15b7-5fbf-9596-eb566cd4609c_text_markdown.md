# What Makes Quant Research Trustworthy? A Research-Referee Specification for an AI Quant Researcher

Experienced practitioners reject quantitative research mainly because they cannot tell how many things were tried, whether every input was actually knowable at decision time, and whether the result survives costs and fresh data. A deterministic "referee" that enforces point-in-time data, counts trials, seals holdouts and forces live forward validation is therefore necessary for AI-generated research to deserve senior attention. The evidence does not show that it is a sufficient, defensible standalone product, because leading firms are building it in-house and parts of it already ship in existing platforms.

## TL;DR

- **The failure modes are well documented, and they get worse as the number of trials grows.** On 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe ratios predicted out-of-sample Sharpe with R² < 0.025, and more backtesting meant a bigger gap. An AI researcher that can run thousands of trials makes this worse unless trial counting and holdout sealing sit outside its control.
- **The core referee controls are known and mostly automatable.** They are point-in-time snapshots, purged and embargoed splits, trial registries feeding the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, sealed holdouts, versioned lineage, cost models and prospective shadow tracking. Economic rationale, methodology approval, holdout release and capital decisions must stay with humans.
- **Verdict: MIXED.** Man Group's AlphaGPT shows the pattern works. Bloomberg (10 July 2025) quoted Man Numeric's Ziang Fang as saying "several dozen signals generated by the AI system have passed Man Group's investment committee and are slated to be deployed in live trading," and Man says they pass "the same evaluation thresholds required for human-generated research." But it was built in-house. QuantConnect, Numerai, WorldQuant BRAIN, ArcticDB, KX and mlfinlab already cover many of the controls. The defensible opening is narrower: a referee plus research memory for mid-sized and energy/commodity desks that lack in-house infrastructure. This has to be confirmed in interviews before building.

## 1. Executive conclusion

**What practitioners require (FACT, drawn from academic and regulatory sources):**

1. **An ex-ante economic rationale.** Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz's "A Backtesting Protocol in the Era of Machine Learning" (Journal of Financial Data Science, Winter 2019) puts research motivation first. Their cautionary example, "S(3)−U(3)", goes long stocks whose ticker has "S" as the third letter and short those with "U". It shows "nearly 6% alpha a year, and statistically significant… This strategy might seem too good to be true. And it is."
2. **Full disclosure of the number of trials.** Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu state that "a backtest that does not report the number of trials N makes it impossible to assess the risk of overfitting."
3. **Significance hurdles that reflect how much has been mined.** Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new factor needs a t-statistic above 3.0 and that "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false."
4. **Independent "effective challenge" and ongoing outcomes analysis.** This is the language of SR 11-7 and PRA SS1/23.
5. **Evidence that the result holds prospectively.** McLean and Pontiff find that published predictors earn 26% less out-of-sample and 58% less post-publication.

**Where the product thesis stands (INFERENCE):**

- The "referee" layer is the most defensible part of the AI-quant-researcher concept, because AI raises the volume of hypotheses and so makes the multiple-testing problem worse.
- Man Group says this openly. AlphaGPT's "speed can create specific statistical risks," and its builders "ran into a lot of issues—hallucination, lookahead bias, multiple testing."
- Man's response was to enforce its existing research methodology, not to buy a tool. That is the central threat to the thesis: sophisticated firms treat the referee as part of their own proprietary research process.

**Recommendation:**

- Do **not** build a general "AI junior quant" for top-tier systematic funds.
- Consider a **referee-first product with research memory** for smaller teams, particularly power/gas/commodity desks, where point-in-time handling of forecast vintages and publication delays is hard and in-house platforms are thinner.
- Validate that demand in interviews before writing code. Treat it as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 2. Top 20 research failure modes

Labels: FACT = directly sourced; INFERENCE = reasoned; ANECDOTE = practitioner statement.

| # | Failure mode | Mechanism | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multiple testing / data snooping | The best of N noisy trials looks significant | Bailey & López de Prado (Deflated Sharpe Ratio): after about 1,000 independent trials the expected maximum Sharpe is about 3.26 in standardised units even when the true Sharpe is zero | FACT |
| 2 | Backtest over-optimisation | Too many variations for too little data | Bailey et al.: with 5 years of daily data, no more than 45 strategy variations should be tried (Minimum Backtest Length) | FACT |
| 3 | Backtest-to-live disconnect | Headline metrics don't predict live results | Wiecki et al. (Quantopian, 888 algorithms): backtest Sharpe R² < 0.025 against out-of-sample; more backtesting, bigger gap | FACT |
| 4 | Published-anomaly fragility | Microcaps, equal-weighting, weak hurdles | Hou, Xue & Zhang: 65% of 452 anomalies fail \|t\|≥1.96; 82% fail at 2.78; 96% of trading-frictions anomalies fail | FACT (contested, see Caveats) |
| 5 | Signal decay / crowding | Arbitrage after discovery | McLean & Pontiff: 97 predictors, −26% out-of-sample, −58% post-publication | FACT |
| 6 | Look-ahead bias (classic) | Using information not yet available | Treated as foundational in López de Prado's "10 Reasons Most ML Funds Fail" and the Arnott et al. protocol | FACT |
| 7 | Look-ahead bias inside LLM weights | The model has memorised the future | Sarkar & Vafa: Llama 2 mentions Covid-19 in over 25% of cases when asked about risks in Sep–Nov 2019 earnings calls; DatedGPT finds a significant "lookahead premium" for models whose training covers the outcome period | FACT |
| 8 | Revised-data leakage | Using today's revised macro numbers | ALFRED stores every vintage; e.g., Q1-1947 real GDP has 14 published versions | FACT |
| 9 | Publication-delay errors | Data timestamped by delivery period, not release time | ENTSO-E realised values appear hours after delivery and balancing data later still; forecasts must be archived at publication time | ANECDOTE (practitioner vendor blog) |
| 10 | Point-in-time fundamentals errors | Restated vendor data | ArcticDB's bitemporal "as_of" design exists to reproduce "the data as it was when we made the decision" | FACT (vendor) |
| 11 | Survivorship bias | Delisted or dead instruments dropped | Hou-Xue-Zhang adjust for CRSP delisting returns; FINSABER-style LLM benchmarks explicitly track survivorship | FACT |
| 12 | Universe-selection bias | Universe chosen after seeing results | Microcap and breakpoint choices materially change conclusions (Hou-Xue-Zhang) | FACT/INFERENCE |
| 13 | Target leakage / label overlap | Overlapping label windows in cross-validation | López de Prado: purging and embargo required because standard CV leaks | FACT |
| 14 | Hyperparameter tuning on test data | The test set becomes a training set | Numerai warns that training on validation data means in-sample metrics won't "generalize out-of-sample" | FACT |
| 15 | Signal-combination overfitting | Picking the best k of n signals | Novy-Marx: combining the best k of n random signals produces large spurious t-statistics | FACT (secondary summary) |
| 16 | Regime cherry-picking | Regime defined after the fact | Practitioner guidance: regimes must be defined by mechanism, not by backtest results | ANECDOTE |
| 17 | Unrealistic costs, fills and impact | Frictions omitted | Hou-Xue-Zhang: replicated anomalies are "much smaller than originally reported"; liquidity anomalies fail most often | FACT/INFERENCE |
| 18 | Non-stationarity / model decay | Relationships shift | SR 11-7 and SS1/23 require ongoing monitoring because "assumptions about the continued relationship between variables in historical data" can fail | FACT |
| 19 | Implementation / engine risk and irreproducibility | Same strategy, different engine, different result | A 2026 arXiv paper on "implementation risk" quantifies divergence across backtest engines | FACT (preprint) |
| 20 | Undocumented manual intervention / spec drift | What was tested is not what was claimed | Man: the AI "might conceptualise one research idea but implement something different"; SEC Marketing Rule cases included failures to retain records of advertised hypothetical performance | FACT |

Also relevant but folded into the rows above: p-hacking (rows 1 and 14), overlap and dependence errors in inference (row 13), unstable feature importance (INFERENCE: a symptom of rows 1 and 18; no strong public source found), and incorrect statistical inference from non-normal returns. The DSR explicitly corrects for skewness and kurtosis.

**Regulatory angle (FACT):** The SEC's Marketing Rule sweeps charged nine advisers in September 2023 ($850,000 combined) and five in April 2024 ($200,000 combined) over hypothetical and back-tested performance. The charges concerned missing policies and procedures, and in some cases recordkeeping. They did not concern statistical methodology. INFERENCE: regulators police the *governance* of backtests (documentation, audience, records) far more than the statistics, so a referee's audit trail has compliance value even where its statistics add little.

## 3. How senior quants detect bad research

**Evidence quality warning:** Public, primary evidence of how senior quants at specific firms actually review junior work is thin. What exists comes from (a) published protocols by practitioner-academics (Arnott/Harvey/Markowitz; López de Prado; Bailey; CFM's Rej, Seager and Bouchaud on discounting backtest PnL), (b) regulatory model-validation guidance, and (c) one detailed firm disclosure (Man Group AlphaGPT). Everything below maps these sources to review questions. Specific firm practices remain **UNKNOWN**.

**Universal review questions (supported by multiple sources):**

| Question | Why it's asked | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Why should this relationship exist? | Theory-free patterns are usually false | Arnott et al. protocol; López de Prado ("a strategy that lacks a theoretical justification is likely to be false"); Man: "every AI-produced signal must demonstrate clear economic rationale" |
| Was this knowable at the time? | Look-ahead and revised data | ALFRED/ArcticDB design; LLM look-ahead literature |
| How many things did you try, including the ones you didn't show me? | Selection bias | DSR; Bailey et al. on reporting N; Wiecki et al. |
| What does it beat? | Weak benchmarks inflate results | JKP argue anomalies must be judged on risk-adjusted alpha, not raw returns; RD-Agent(Q) benchmarks against Alpha158/Alpha360 |
| What happens after costs, capacity and impact? | Frictions kill small-cap and liquidity effects | Hou-Xue-Zhang |
| Which periods or names drive the result? | Concentration and regime dependence | INFERENCE from the non-stationarity literature |
| Does it survive alternative definitions, universes and weightings? | Specification search | Hou-Xue-Zhang's value-weighting and breakpoint choices |
| Is it new, or a repackaged known factor? | Crowding and redundancy | WorldQuant BRAIN correlation tests; AlphaGPT/AlphaAgent originality checks; JKP's 13 factor themes |
| How will we know when it dies? | Decay monitoring | SR 11-7 ongoing monitoring; McLean-Pontiff decay |
| Can someone else reproduce it from the record? | Irreproducibility | SS1/23: documentation sufficient for an independent third party to "replicate" results |

**Firm-specific (UNKNOWN or ANECDOTE):** actual Sharpe or t-stat hurdles, holdout length, shadow-trading duration, committee composition, and whether trial counts are formally logged. Man discloses only that AI signals must pass "identical thresholds" to human signals and go through Investment Committee review plus code review. It does not disclose the thresholds themselves.

**Sophisticated firms vs. smaller teams (INFERENCE):**
- Large firms (Man, and presumably others of similar scale) have proprietary research platforms, versioned data (ArcticDB began at Man), investment committees and technology review.
- Smaller teams, and energy desks inside utilities and trading houses, are more likely to rely on notebooks, vendor data and individual judgement. That makes them both more exposed to these failure modes and more likely to lack a referee.
- This is the single most important claim to test in interviews.

## 4. Trust checklist

A result deserves a senior quant's attention only if the answer to every item is "yes, with stored evidence":

1. **Pre-registration:** the hypothesis, economic mechanism, target, universe, horizon and primary metric were registered *before* the first run.
2. **Point-in-time integrity:** every input is read from an immutable snapshot "as of" the decision timestamp, including vintage and publication time, not just the observation period.
3. **LLM contamination check:** if an LLM produced features or forecasts, the evaluation period falls after the model's knowledge cutoff, or a time-bounded model was used.
4. **Label availability:** labels and targets are purged and embargoed so no training label overlaps the test windows.
5. **Trial accounting:** the full count of trials in the hypothesis family (including abandoned variants) is recorded, and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio or an equivalent haircut uses it.
6. **Hurdle:** the evidence clears a pre-set hurdle adjusted for the family's search intensity (e.g., t > 3 for new factor-type claims, per Harvey-Liu-Zhu).
7. **Baselines:** the result beats pre-specified strong baselines (known factor models, naive and seasonal forecasts, the existing production book) on the same data and costs.
8. **Costs:** results are reported gross and net under a firm-approved cost, fill and impact model, with sensitivity to that model.
9. **Robustness:** alternative definitions, subperiods, universes and weightings are reported, *including the failures*.
10. **Sealed holdout:** a single, logged, first-and-only evaluation on data the researcher (human or AI) never touched.
11. **Reproducibility:** code hash, data snapshot IDs, environment and random seeds allow a bit-for-bit re-run.
12. **Novelty:** correlation to existing signals is measured and below the firm threshold.
13. **Kill criteria:** decay and invalidation conditions are defined before any shadow trading.

## 5. Deterministic referee specification

Design principle: the referee is a separate service with its own identity and permissions. The AI researcher calls it but cannot write to its stores, change its configuration or see sealed data. Each control below lists what it protects against, whether it can be automated, whether it must be immutable, and what audit evidence it stores.

| Control | Protects against | Automatable? | Immutable? | Audit evidence stored |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1. Point-in-time snapshot service** (bitemporal: observation time, knowledge time) | Look-ahead, revised data, restatements, publication delay | Yes, once per-source publication-time metadata exists; building that metadata is manual | Yes: snapshots are append-only (ArcticDB-style `as_of`/snapshots; ALFRED vintages) | Snapshot ID, source, vintage, publication-lag table version |
| **R2. Publication-lag registry** (per dataset and document type, e.g., TSO forecasts, balancing data, weather model runs) | Timestamp and publication-delay errors | Partially: lags must be curated and verified empirically | Versioned; changes need human approval | Lag table history, who approved changes |
| **R3. Train/validation/test boundary enforcer** with purging and embargo | Target leakage, overlap errors | Yes | Boundaries are fixed per registered study | Split definitions, purge and embargo parameters |
| **R4. Label-availability check** | Using labels before they are realised | Yes | Yes | Label realisation timestamps against feature timestamps |
| **R5. Hypothesis-family registry** | p-hacking, silent re-framing | Yes (a form plus hash) | Yes after registration; amendments create a new version with a visible diff | Pre-registration document, hash, timestamp |
| **R6. Trial counter** (every run, parameter set and abandoned idea, logged by the referee rather than self-reported) | Selection bias, hidden trials | Yes, *if all compute goes through the referee* | Yes (append-only ledger) | Full trial ledger, correlation clusters for effective N |
| **R7. Multiple-testing correction** (DSR, PBO via combinatorial purged CV, Harvey-Liu haircuts, Holm/BHY across a family) | Data snooping | Yes | Method set by policy, not by the agent | Raw and adjusted statistics, N used, method version |
| **R8. Sealed holdout vault** | Tuning on test data | Yes | Yes: one-shot release, logged, requires human key | Access log, single evaluation result |
| **R9. Baseline competence suite** | Weak benchmarks | Mostly: baselines configured per domain by humans | Baseline set frozen per study | Baseline results side by side |
| **R10. Mutation / poisoning tests** (shuffle labels, shift features forward one period, inject future data, randomise tickers) | Hidden leakage; pipelines that "find" signal in noise | Yes | Test suite versioned | Test outcomes; a pass means performance *collapses* under mutation |
| **R11. Transaction cost, fill and impact model** | Unrealistic costs and fills | Yes, but model calibration is human-owned | Firm-approved version pinned | Gross and net PnL, cost-model version, sensitivities |
| **R12. Code/data/model versioning and lineage** | Irreproducibility, undocumented intervention | Yes (git hashes, container digests, MLflow/DVC-style tracking) | Yes | Full lineage graph from hypothesis to result |
| **R13. Independent replication** (re-run in a clean environment, ideally on a second engine) | Engine and implementation risk, spec drift | Yes for re-runs; a second-engine check is partial | Results immutable | Replication diff report |
| **R14. Spec-to-code consistency check** | The AI implements something other than the claim | Partially: LLM-assisted plus human code review (Man uses both) | n/a | Reviewer sign-off, diff between spec and implementation |
| **R15. Live forward (shadow) validation** | Overfitting, decay | Yes | Track record permanent: "a live track record is only meaningful if it cannot be selectively erased" (Numerai) | Timestamped predictions committed before outcomes, realised scores |
| **R16. Novelty/redundancy check** | Crowding, rediscovering known factors | Yes | Reference library versioned | Correlation to production and library signals |

**Domain note for power/gas (INFERENCE, backed by ANECDOTE):**
- R1 and R2 are where energy desks most need help. The ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (mandated by EU Regulation 543/2013, live since 5 January 2015) is "a regulatory publication, not a trading feed… late by design," with per-document publication delays, according to a practitioner vendor.
- Hirth, Mühlenpfordt and Bulkeley's review of the platform (Applied Energy, vol. 225, September 2018) reports: "We find mixed results and identify a range of shortcomings… Our suggestions include improving the governance structure and giving users more say."
- Weather-forecast and TSO-forecast vintages have to be archived at publication time, because later downloads may not reproduce what was known.

## 6. Controls the AI must not own

After seeing any result, the AI researcher must never be able to alter:

1. The registered hypothesis, primary metric, target definition, horizon or universe (only a new, visibly linked registration is allowed).
2. Train/test/holdout boundaries, purge and embargo parameters, or holdout access.
3. The trial ledger: it cannot delete, merge or relabel trials, and cannot choose the "effective N."
4. The multiple-testing method or significance hurdle.
5. The baseline set.
6. The cost, fill and impact model or its parameters.
7. Point-in-time snapshots, the publication-lag registry or data vintages.
8. Mutation-test definitions.
9. The shadow track record, or the kill criteria set before shadow trading.
10. Its own permissions, the referee's code or configuration, and promotion decisions.

Rationale (INFERENCE): each of these is a degree of freedom that turns a test into a search. The AI's incentive to find something that "works" is structurally the same as the human incentive Man describes ("quant researchers want to show something that works, at least on paper"). An LLM agent is also optimised against feedback. RD-Agent(Q) uses a multi-armed bandit over directions, so any referee output the agent can see becomes an optimisation target.

## 7. Promotion gates: research → shadow → production

Thresholds below are **configurable defaults proposed as INFERENCE**. No public source gives industry-standard values.

| Gate | Entry requirement | Who decides | Evidence package |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G0 Read-worthy** (reaches a human) | Registered hypothesis with rationale; checks R1–R4, R10 and R12 pass; beats baselines net of costs; DSR above a family threshold; novelty below the correlation cap | Automated referee, then a human triage queue | Auto-generated one-page "referee report": claim, N trials, adjusted statistics, failures, lineage |
| **G1 Worth human research time** | G0 plus robustness grid; subperiod stability; no single period or name dominates; replication (R13) passes | Senior quant | Report plus reviewer notes |
| **G2 Shadow trading** | Sealed-holdout evaluation (R8) passes; spec-to-code review; kill criteria and minimum shadow length fixed in advance (Minimum Track Record Length logic) | Senior quant plus independent validator/risk | Holdout log; pre-committed shadow plan |
| **G3 Production** | Shadow results inside the backtest's statistical envelope (QuantConnect's Mia uses this same "must land inside the backtest's statistical envelope" test); capacity and impact review; operational checks | PM plus risk plus investment committee | Full lineage, shadow record, approval signatures |
| **G4 Monitoring / retirement** | Ongoing outcomes analysis; automatic alerts when kill criteria are breached | Risk / model validation | Decay dashboard; SR 11-7-style outcomes analysis |

Man's disclosed process fits G1–G3: AI signals enter "the standard research and investment committee process" with "dual-track validation" (committee review of hypothesis and rationale, plus technology code review, unit and integration tests, and scenario analysis).

## 8. Limits of automation

- **Economic rationale cannot be fully automated.** AlphaAgent uses LLM-judged "hypothesis-factor alignment," but that only checks that the code matches the story. It doesn't check that the story is true. INFERENCE: an LLM can generate a plausible story for almost any pattern after the fact, so a rationale must be timestamped *before* results.
- **Effective N is a judgement call.** Correlated trials make the literal count an upper bound. Clustering methods (e.g., the ONC algorithm) estimate effective N, but the choice of method affects the answer.
- **Publication-lag metadata needs domain curation.** It is especially messy for European power data and weather vintages.
- **Cost and impact models need live execution data.** Most smaller firms lack enough of it to calibrate (UNKNOWN extent).
- **Research off the platform is invisible.** A trial counter only counts what runs through it. Ideas a human explored in a private notebook, or that were "known" from prior work, are unlogged. This is the knowledge-contamination problem.
- **LLM parametric look-ahead can't be audited through the data pipeline.** It "lives inside the model's weights and is invisible to data-pipeline audits" (2026 arXiv preprint). The only clean remedies are time-bounded models or evaluation periods after the model's cutoff, and the latter shrinks the usable history.
- **The statistical framework is contested.** Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen (Journal of Finance, 2023) find most factors *do* replicate under a Bayesian framework. A 2022 paper argues "most claimed statistical findings in cross-sectional return predictability are likely true." Hard-coding one school (e.g., "t > 3 always") is a methodological choice, not an objective truth.

## 9. How the referee itself could fail (red team)

| Risk | How it happens | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too restrictive** | Frequentist haircuts reject real but modest signals; JKP show Bayesian approaches reach opposite conclusions | High for adoption | Pluggable methods chosen by firm policy; report several corrections side by side |
| **Statistically naive** | Literal trial counts overstate N; DSR assumptions misapplied to overlapping or non-IID returns | Medium | Effective-N estimation; human-approved method per family |
| **Slow** | Pre-registration and holdout ceremony block exploration | High | Two modes: a free "sandbox" whose results are *never* promotable, and a registered "confirmatory" track |
| **Incompatible with creative research** | Exploratory work gets forced through confirmatory gates | Medium | Keep exploration and confirmation formally separate (the sandbox/confirmatory split) |
| **Easy to game** | The agent re-registers slight variants as "new" families; it learns the referee's thresholds via feedback; it trains on data it isn't supposed to see through proxies | High | Similarity checks across families (AST/correlation, as in AlphaAgent/BRAIN); coarse, delayed feedback on holdout-adjacent metrics; count family-level trials |
| **Redundant with existing infrastructure** | Firms already have ArcticDB/kdb+, MLflow, internal committees | **Very high for sophisticated firms** | Sell only where that infrastructure is missing; integrate with it rather than replacing it |
| **False sense of security** | Referee-passed research is treated as validated; model-risk teams warn about "validation in name only" | High | The referee report states what it *cannot* check (rationale, off-platform knowledge, LLM contamination) |
| **Vendor-model problem** | SR 11-7 expects third-party models to be validated with equal rigour, but vendor opacity makes that hard | Medium | Open-source the referee's statistical core so it can be validated |

## 10. MVP referee: minimum controls needed for credibility

Credibility means a senior quant would accept the referee's report as a *filter*, not as approval. Minimum set:

1. **R1 plus R2:** a point-in-time snapshot layer with a publication-lag registry (with vendor/TSO forecast vintages for energy customers).
2. **R5 plus R6:** pre-registration and an append-only trial ledger, with all agent compute routed through the referee.
3. **R7:** DSR, PBO and a Holm/BHY family correction, with the method chosen by firm policy.
4. **R8:** a sealed holdout with human-keyed, one-shot release.
5. **R10:** mutation tests (future-shift, label-shuffle).
6. **R11:** a pinned, firm-approved cost model; gross and net always reported.
7. **R12:** full lineage (code hash, data snapshot, environment).
8. **R15:** a permanent, timestamped shadow record.
9. **An auto-generated referee report** that lists N trials, adjusted statistics, failures and explicit blind spots.

Defer: second-engine replication, automated rationale scoring, impact-model calibration, novelty against proprietary libraries.

**Competitive reality check (FACT, from vendor sources):**
- **QuantConnect:** managers can enforce an organisation-wide out-of-sample period ("researchers on your team will not be able to use the most recent year of data"). It also has a parameter-detection system, and its agent Mia gates ideas by "statistical validation… backtests with realistic reality modeling, then out-of-sample paper trading that must land inside the backtest's statistical envelope."
- **Numerai:** scores only live predictions and keeps a permanent record.
- **WorldQuant BRAIN:** scores only alphas that "qualify for out-of-sample testing" and removes correlated alphas.
- **ArcticDB:** provides bitemporal time travel and snapshots.
- **KX:** launched "Trading Signal Agents" blueprints with NVIDIA in March 2026, promising point-in-time correct, "governed outputs."
- **Hudson & Thames' MlFinLab:** ships deflated and haircut Sharpe ratios and minimum track record length.
- **ValidMind:** sells SR 11-7/SS1/23 model-risk documentation to banks.
- **SigTech:** a 2019 Brevan Howard spin-out that marketed MAGIC agents "like working with a junior quant researcher," has closed. WatersTechnology (10 March 2026, "SigTech's closure amid agentic AI boom raises questions") reports that "sources say competition from leading AI companies was too stiff to combat."
- **Gap:** in the sources reviewed, no product clearly combines automatic, referee-owned trial counting feeding DSR/PBO, a holdout sealed *from an LLM agent*, and structured research memory of failures. This is based on a non-exhaustive scan (Deltix/EPAM, W&B/MLflow-in-finance and several startups were not verified).

## Caveats

- Private workflows at Two Sigma, Jane Street, Citadel, G-Research, HRT, Winton, Cubist and similar firms are not publicly documented in enough detail. Claims about "what senior quants ask" are reconstructed from protocols and regulation, not observed.
- The replication debate is live. Hou-Xue-Zhang (high failure rates) and Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen (high replication) reach opposite conclusions from different methods. A referee has to encode one position or let the firm choose.
- SR 11-7 and SS1/23 apply to banks. SS1/23 applies specifically to banks and PRA-designated firms with internal-model approval. Hedge funds, prop firms and most commodity traders are not directly subject to them. Their value here is as a *template* for independent challenge, not as a compliance driver.
- RD-Agent(Q)'s "2× returns with 70% fewer factors" is a self-reported research result on CSI 300 by the tool's authors. The authors caution that users should "use the agent-generated code, data, and model with caution" and that the framework does not "replace the role of qualified financial professionals." It is not independent evidence of live value.
- Man's AlphaGPT figures come from Bloomberg's 10 July 2025 report and Man's own marketing. In that report, the "several dozen" signals had passed the investment committee and were "slated to be deployed in live trading," so at the time they were not confirmed live. Their live performance is undisclosed.
- Energy-data publication-delay claims rest partly on a practitioner vendor's blog (ANECDOTE) and a 2018 academic review. They need checking with desk interviews.

## Implications for the AI Quant Researcher Thesis

**1. What the evidence supports**
- AI-driven research raises the multiple-testing risk. Both an adopter (Man Group) and the academic literature (DSR, Minimum Backtest Length, Quantopian's 888-algorithm study) say so directly. An independent control plane is therefore a real need, not a cosmetic one.
- The pattern of "AI proposes, deterministic process judges, humans approve" is already how the most prominent institutional adopter works. Man subjects AI signals to "identical thresholds," dual-track validation and full decision logging.
- The required controls are well specified in the literature and mostly automatable (Section 5).
- LLM-specific look-ahead bias is a new, well-documented failure mode (2024–2026) that older infrastructure does not address. It creates a genuinely new control requirement.
- Energy/commodity point-in-time problems (forecast vintages, TSO publication delays) are real and poorly served by generic equity tooling. INFERENCE, needs validation.

**2. What the evidence weakens**
- **Redundancy:** sophisticated firms build this in-house and see it as their edge. Man says "our sustainable edge lies in… research philosophy refined through multiple market cycles" and "early-mover technology advantages are inherently temporary."
- **Existing products:** QuantConnect (holdout enforcement plus the Mia agent with evidence gates), Numerai/WorldQuant BRAIN (sealed live evaluation) and ArcticDB/KX (point-in-time) already cover large parts of the referee.
- **Commoditisation:** the "junior quant" agent layer is commoditising. RD-Agent is open-source and runs experiments for under $10; AlphaAgent is published; Man expects "widespread adoption."
- **No compliance driver:** regulation forces governance at banks, not at hedge funds or trading houses. The enforcement that does exist (SEC Marketing Rule) targets advertising and records, not research statistics.
- **Methodological disagreement:** the statistical foundations are contested, so a "deterministic" referee risks encoding one school's view.

**3. What remains unknown and cannot be resolved through desk research**
- Whether mid-sized funds and energy desks actually lack these controls, or already have adequate home-grown ones.
- Who owns the budget (head of quant research, CRO, CTO) and whether a referee is a line item or a feature request.
- Whether firms will let an external system see their data, trial histories and failures. Research memory is the most sensitive IP they have.
- The actual promotion thresholds, holdout conventions and shadow durations inside firms.
- Whether AI-generated signals at firms like Man perform live in line with human ones.
- Whether senior quants would trust a *vendor's* referee report, or would re-do the checks anyway.

**4. The 5 most important questions to ask practitioners**
1. "Walk me through the last piece of research you rejected. What exactly killed it, and at what stage?"
2. "Today, how do you know how many variants a researcher tried before showing you a result? Is that logged anywhere, or is it trust?"
3. "How do you guarantee point-in-time correctness for your hardest dataset, e.g., forecast vintages, TSO publications or restated fundamentals? What has gone wrong?"
4. "If an AI agent generated 500 hypotheses a week, what would break first in your review process, and who would you want enforcing the rules?"
5. "Would you buy a control plane from outside, or does this have to be built in-house? What would a vendor need to prove, and who would sign the contract?"

**5. Verdict: MIXED**

The need is real and getting sharper with AI, and the referee specification is buildable. But the strongest buyers are building it themselves, and credible pieces already ship elsewhere. So the broad "AI quant researcher for professional trading firms" thesis is **weakened**. A narrower **referee-first product with research memory** for mid-sized systematic teams and power/gas/commodity desks is **plausible but unproven**.

Proceed only to customer discovery, not to building. If interviews show that these desks already have trial logging, point-in-time vintages and holdout discipline, or will not let an outside system hold their research memory, the verdict becomes **DO NOT BUILD**.
